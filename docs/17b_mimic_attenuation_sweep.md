# 17b. How much attenuation before the lists part company?

Generated by `17b_mimic_attenuation_sweep.py`. Do not edit by hand.

`17` fixed the efficacy shape at three chosen values. This sweeps it continuously and reports the point at which divergence begins, which is a threshold rather than a choice.

ARR(p) = p (1-p)^beta, beta from 0 to 4 in steps of 0.05. beta 0 is proportional benefit and gives identical lists by construction. The benefit peak sits at p* = 1 / (1 + beta). The scale constant is dropped because both reported quantities are invariant to it.

## Two onsets

| Onset | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Jaccard below 0.80 | the lists name substantially different people |
| Shortfall above 5% | that difference costs measurable outcomes |

`17` showed these are not the same point. Reporting only the first overstates the case. Reporting only the second hides that a different population is being served for the same result.

A programme that believes benefit is close to proportional should rank by risk and this whole comparison is moot for them. A programme that believes benefit falls away at the top of the risk distribution can read off the attenuation at which their two options separate, and separately the attenuation at which the separation starts costing outcomes. Neither number is an estimate of their efficacy. Both are conditional on a belief they hold and this data cannot supply.
