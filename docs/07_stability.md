# 07. Ranking stability

Generated by `07_stability.py`. Do not edit by hand.

`06_allocation.md` found that two risk models fit on resamples of the same cohort agree on only 8% of their top 1%. That floor absorbed most of the measured divergence between risk ranking and effect ranking. This document establishes whether the floor is a property of risk based allocation or a property of the estimator used to produce it.

Three estimators, 10 replications each. Two independent bootstrap resamples per replication, top k sets compared.

## Day 0, cohort CCC 2014J, N = 2,285

| bagged | 0.23 | 0.22 | 0.32 | 0.40 | 0.56 | 0.930 |
| logistic | 0.53 | 0.54 | 0.60 | 0.65 | 0.72 | 0.676 |

### Score spread in the top decile

- Median predicted risk: 0.7762
- Range: 0.7132 to 0.9330
- Interquartile range: 0.0829

If the interquartile range here is small relative to the resampling variation in individual predictions, the students at the top are close to tied and no estimator can order them reliably.

## Which reading holds

If bagging raises agreement substantially without costing AUC, the floor in 06 was an artifact of estimator variance. The allocation analysis should then be rerun on bagged predictions and the divergence reassessed against the lower floor.

If bagging changes little, and the logistic model is no better despite far lower variance, then instability is intrinsic. In that case the more important finding is not that effect ranking beats risk ranking. It is that a risk ranked priority list is not reproducible at the capacities institutions actually operate at, which undermines the practice regardless of which ranking is theoretically correct.
