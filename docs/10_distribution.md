# 10. Distribution

Generated by `10_distribution.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Effect ranking under a saturating efficacy assumption moves attention away from the highest risk people by design. This document measures who is dropped, what is given up in coverage of people who actually withdraw, and whether the composition of the selected group shifts.

Nothing here is a fairness verdict. Whether a shift is acceptable is a policy question that depends on what an institution owes to whom, and this analysis cannot settle it. It can make the shift visible, which is the minimum owed to anyone asked to adopt the recommendation.

All predictions are out of fold on the folds fixed in `02_cohort.py`. No randomness.

## How to read this

The dropped group is by construction the highest risk part of the list. Q1 gives the size of that gap and Q2 gives what it costs in coverage of eventual withdrawals. Neither is an argument against effect ranking. Both are facts that have to accompany the recommendation, because an institution adopting it is choosing to contact fewer of the students most likely to leave, and should do so knowingly.

Compositional shifts in Q3 are descriptive. A shift away from the most deprived band would be a serious matter; a shift towards it would not automatically be a good one. Either way the number belongs in front of whoever makes the decision.
